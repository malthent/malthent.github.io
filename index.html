Anders Malthe-Sørenssen

Professor of physics. I use computational physics and machine learning to build a physics-based understanding of complex systems, from friction at the atomic scale to learning in brains and in artificial neural networks, and I work to make computing a natural part of how science is taught.

I am Vice-President of the Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters and direct two Norwegian Centres for Excellence in Education at the University of Oslo: the Center for Interdisciplinary Education (INTED) and the Center for Computing in Science Education (CCSE). I also chaired the Norwegian government's expert committee on artificial intelligence in higher education, which delivered its report NOU 2026: 14 in November 2026.

Research

My work is cross-disciplinary. The common thread is method: molecular dynamics, statistical physics models and machine learning, applied where a physical description can explain how a system behaves.

AI and neuroscience

How knowledge of the brain can improve artificial intelligence, and how learning in artificial systems can tell us about the brain. Recent work covers grid cells and place cells in trained networks, and how AI models form internal representations, including protein language models.

Friction and materials design

Atomic-scale simulations of friction, wear and fracture, and machine-learning methods for designing surfaces with chosen frictional properties, such as graphene kirigami with a negative friction coefficient.

Nanoscale geological processes

How fluids, reactions and fracture interact in rocks at the nanoscale, with applications to earthquakes and slow rupture, glacier surges, metamorphism and the storage of CO2.

Computing in science education

Discipline-based education research on integrating computation into science courses from the first semester: computational essays, student agency, and machine-learning tools for education research at scale.

Books

Springer · Undergraduate Texts in Physics · 2026

Elementary Electromagnetism Using Python

A modern introductory course that combines analytical and numerical techniques. It runs from electric fields and potentials through Gauss' law, dielectrics and conductors to magnetic fields, Faraday's law and Maxwell's equations, with computation built into the examples and exercises.

Minilectures, exercises and exams → Springer

Springer · Lecture Notes in Physics 1029 · 2024 · Open access

Percolation Theory Using Python

A graduate text on disorder, scaling and fractal geometry: percolation in one and infinite dimensions, cluster structure, scaling theory, and diffusion and flow in disordered media, with complete Python code for the worked examples.

Springer

Springer · Undergraduate Lecture Notes in Physics · 2015

Elementary Mechanics Using Python

An introductory mechanics textbook that combines analytical and numerical methods from the first chapter, so students can work on realistic problems. Also published in a parallel edition, Elementary Mechanics Using Matlab.

Leadership and service

Current
Vice-President, Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters. Det Norske Videnskaps-Akademi (DNVA) is Norway's national academy of science, founded in 1857.
2023–2027
Director, Center for Interdisciplinary Education (INTED). A Centre for Excellence in Education at the University of Oslo for 2023–2027. INTED works to strengthen interdisciplinary competence among students and staff.
2025–2026
Chair, government expert committee on artificial intelligence in higher education. Appointed by the Norwegian government in March 2025 and known as the Malthe-Sørenssen-utvalget. The committee delivered its report, NOU 2026: 14 «Kunstig intelligens i høyere utdanning – I kunnskapens tjeneste», to the Ministry of Education and Research in November 2026.
2016–2026
Director, Center for Computing in Science Education (CCSE). A Centre for Excellence in Education hosted by the Department of Physics at the University of Oslo, working to make computing a natural tool for all science and engineering students from their first semester.
Ongoing
Founder and leader of the Honours programme at UiO and of the CompSci doctoral programme. Interdisciplinary programmes for undergraduate and doctoral training in computational and data science.
2003–2013
Group leader, Physics of Geological Processes (PGP). A Norwegian Centre of Excellence at the University of Oslo.
Industry
Research commercialisation. Several years in a high-tech start-up, industrially financed research projects leading to patented technologies, and technical due-diligence work.
